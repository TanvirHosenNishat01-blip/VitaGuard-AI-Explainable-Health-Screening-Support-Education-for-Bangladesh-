## 1. Biostatistical Calibration Theory & Multi-Dimensional Criteria

In clinical decision support, calibration is fundamentally distinct from discrimination (AUROC). A model with an AUROC of 0.85 can be clinically disastrous if its predicted probabilities are systematically double or triple the patient's true risk.

### The Four Necessary Dimensions of Calibration:
1. **Mean Calibration (Calibration-in-the-Large, CITL):**  
   $$\text{logit}(P(Y=1)) = \alpha + 1 \cdot \text{logit}(\hat{p})$$  
   $\alpha = 0$ indicates perfect agreement between the average predicted probability and true population prevalence.
2. **Weak Calibration (Calibration Slope, $\beta$):**  
   $$\text{logit}(P(Y=1)) = \alpha + \beta \cdot \text{logit}(\hat{p})$$  
   $\beta = 1.0$ indicates that probabilities are spread appropriately across the log-odds scale. $\beta < 1.0$ reflects overconfidence (common in tree ensembles), while $\beta > 1.0$ reflects underconfidence.
3. **Overall Accuracy (Brier Score & Brier Skill Score):**  
   The mean squared error of predicted probabilities. Must be compared against the **Null Prevalence Model** ($\text{Brier}_{\text{null}} = p(1-p)$):
   $$\text{Brier Skill Score (BSS)} = 1 - \frac{\text{Brier}}{\text{Brier}_{\text{null}}}$$
   $\text{BSS} \le 0$ indicates the model is mathematically worse than predicting constant prevalence!
4. **Moderate Calibration (Expected Calibration Error, ECE):**  
   The weighted absolute difference between observed accuracy and confidence across probability bins. **Audit Caveat:** ECE is highly sensitive to bin width and cannot stand alone as evidence of calibration.

### 2.1 Critical Audit Findings on Calibration
1. **The Extreme DiaBD Baseline Distortion:**  
   Uncalibrated tree ensembles and class-weighted logistic models output mean probabilities between **37.9% and 43.1%**, yielding deeply negative Brier Skill Scores ($-2.15$ to $-2.59$). Every uncalibrated model performed far worse than predicting the null prevalence ($0.0593$).
2. **Platt Sigmoid Recovery & Metric Reconciliation:**  
   - *Score Scale & Population:* In Phase 06B, uncalibrated raw score averaged **38.72%** when evaluated on the $N = 1,024$ test split. In Model Excellence, uncalibrated score averaged **37.89%** across development OOF ($N = 4,095$) and **38.53%** on the test split. Both confirm extreme probability shift induced by `scale_pos_weight = 14.81`.
   - *Calibrator Parameters:* Phase 06B fit Platt scaling on decision logits $s = \ln(p/(1-p))$ ($a = 0.76801$, $b = -2.49962$), whereas Model Excellence fit on probabilities $p \in [0, 1]$ ($a = 3.68511$, $b = -4.36905$). Both parameterizations produce mathematically equivalent test-split Brier scores (**0.0572 vs 0.0573**) and calibration slopes (**0.9840 vs 0.9777**), restoring mean predicted risk to **6.40%** (matching 6.35% prevalence).
3. **Mandatory Unblinded Test & Threshold Caveat:**  
   The Platt scaling result on the 1,024-row DiaBD test set is **a historical technical proof-of-concept**, NOT independent external clinical validation. Because this test split was unblinded and explored across cutoffs ($\tau \in [0.04, 0.50]$) in earlier phases, these metrics remain optimistic. **No clinical threshold is approved or authorized.**
4. **Cleveland Calibration: Preliminary & Imprecise Finding:**  
   On the Cleveland cohort, L1-regularized Logistic Regression achieved a calibration slope point estimate of $\beta = 1.0114$, Brier score of $0.1356$, and Brier Skill Score of $+0.4540$. However, this was evaluated on the **development split of only 237 patients (109 events)**. In a sample of this size, sampling error around the slope is wide ($\approx \pm 0.25$). Calling Cleveland the "clinically best-calibrated model" is an overstatement based on an imprecise point estimate. Furthermore, because this cohort comprises tertiary catheterization referrals, it possesses zero validity for primary care screening.

## 4. Methodological Limitations of Bootstrap Implementation

The auditor identifies two critical statistical limitations:
1. **Row-Wise Resampling in Clustered Data:**  
   Row-wise bootstrap assumes independent and identically distributed (i.i.d.) observations. If the DiaBD survey data contains multi-stage cluster sampling across 63 unions (as proven in Phase 06C with $\text{DEFF} = 1.30$), standard row-wise bootstrap produces **confidence intervals that are approximately 15% too narrow**. Cluster-level block bootstrap was not evaluable due to missing union cluster identifiers in the open Mendeley dataset.
2. **No Generation of External Validity:**  
   Bootstrap resampling estimates sampling variability *within the source dataset distribution*. It does NOT estimate transportability to new hospitals, new districts, or distinct clinical case-mixes.
